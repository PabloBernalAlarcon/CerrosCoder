# Choose a problem worth solving

**Project, session 1 of 5.** For the next five weeks, you will build **your own program**, for a problem **you** choose.

## What makes a program useful?

Think about three programs from this year: the **quiz engine**, the **password checker** and the **score analyser**. What made each one useful? Who was it for?

Then look at your "I can" sheets. List the skills you have now.

## 1. Brainstorm

Write down problems from your own life that a program could help with. Think about school, sport, games, music, or home. For example:

- A tracker for your football team's goals and matches.
- A study quiz for a subject you find hard.
- A budget helper for your weekly spending.
- A game with levels and a high-score list.

## 2. Filter

Your project must:

- [ ] use `input`,
- [ ] use a **condition** (`if`),
- [ ] use a **loop**,
- [ ] use a **list**,
- [ ] use at least one **function** you write,
- [ ] fit in about **four sessions**,
- [ ] be **testable**: you can say what should happen for a given input.

Cross out ideas that don't pass the filter. Too big? Make it smaller!

## 3. Write your proposal

On paper:

1. **The problem**, and **who** the program is for.
2. **Three behaviours** the program must have.
3. **Six tests**, each with the expected behaviour.
4. **The part you are least sure you can build.**

## 4. Peer feedback

Swap proposals with another pair. Give **two strengths** and **one question**.

## How your project is graded

| Criterion | To pass (5), you must… |
|---|---|
| **Problem and design** | Define the problem; your design covers the main flow and functions. |
| **Working program** | Use input, conditions, loops, a list and functions, and work in normal use. |
| **Testing evidence** | Run at least 6 tests, with expected and actual results. |
| **Reflection on learning** | Describe what you learned and what's next. |
| **Readability** | Use reasonable names and readable code. |

## Before you leave

Rate from 1 to 4: **"How sure am I that I can build this?"** You will compare this with the result in week 29. Your proposal goes to your teacher for approval.
